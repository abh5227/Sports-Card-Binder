Round 5 — the page turn

Four ways to get from one spread to the next, inside the minimal frame decided at Round 4. Cards, pockets, gutters, margins, sheen and frame are constant. Only the transition varies. Press the buttons — this is a looking question.

This bar is 150 px — one card. If it does not match, your zoom is not 100%.

Nothing here is decided. Whether one leaf turns or both, whether the back of a turned page shows pockets, how long it takes, whether cards ride through the turn — all of it is open, and each variant answers it differently on purpose. Every panel reports its own worst frame live. The budget is 8.33 ms at 120 Hz.

Duration Applies to every variant below.

T1 — Cross-fade. No flip at all.

The honest baseline: the outgoing spread fades out, the incoming fades in. No 3D, no second face, nothing rotating. If this reads well enough, everything below is cost for nothing — and it is the only variant with no failure mode at the ends of the binder.

T2 — One leaf turns, blank back

The right-hand sheet rotates about the spine and lands on the left. Its reverse is plain page, no pockets — cheap, because only one face is ever rendered. It is also a lie about the object: a real binder page has pockets on both sides.

T3 — One leaf turns, double-sided, cards ride

The faithful one. The turning leaf's reverse is the next spread's left page, with its cards already in their pockets, so the leaf carries real content both ways and the next spread is assembled by the turn rather than after it. Two rendered faces, both populated, plus a moving highlight and a shading pass through the arc.

T4 — Double-sided, but the leaf turns empty and fills on arrival

Same shape as T3. The turning leaf shows pockets but no cards; the cards fade into their pockets once it lands. If T3 cannot hold the frame budget this is the fallback that keeps the physical reading and drops the paint cost.

The ends — there is no spread before the first

Shown on T3. Going back from the first spread has nothing to turn. A refusal has to be visible or the control reads as broken.

Measured — worst frame, not average

Each variant run and instrumented. Spike 01's 36.7 ms figure was a content swap, not a 3D transform with two rendered faces; it does not transfer.
