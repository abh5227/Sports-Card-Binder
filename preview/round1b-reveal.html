Round 1b — where the reveal appears

Bare at rest, information on hover — settled. The only question here is where that information appears. Same content, same typography, same nine cards, same page in all three. Hover any card. The centre card is held open in each so the screenshots capture it.

The grid and frame are scaffolding, not a proposal P2 and P3 cannot be judged without neighbours and a frame, so there has to be a page here. This one is deliberately plain — a flat 3×3 and a flat rectangle. Page layout is Round 3 and the binder object is Round 4. Do not react to the grid or the frame; they are a test rig. Dark-bordered cards dissolving into the surface is known and deferred to Round 2 — it will look wrong here on purpose.
This bar is 150 px — one card wide. If it does not match, your zoom is not 100%.

P1Scrim over the card

A semi-transparent panel over the foot of the card. Text lands where your attention already is, and it behaves identically under a finger on touch. Built sympathetically — sized to its content and translucent rather than a blackout, which is the strongest version of the idea rather than the easiest one to dismiss.

Pinned — two held open at once

A reserved strip in the page margin. Never moves, never occludes, always has room for five lines. The reserved space is permanent — it is shown here in its empty state too, because that emptiness is the standing cost.

Type and colour, carried forward

  • Secondary at 7.5px, tracking 0.10em — up from 6.5px/0.16em, as agreed.
  • --ink-dim #9A968E carries hierarchy (5.74:1 desk / 5.92:1 page). --ledger #A8845C appears only on the serial line.
  • P1's scrim is rgba(8,9,11,.88); --ink on it measures 12.9:1, so the text is AAA even over the brightest card in the set.
  • P3's empty-state placeholder is #4A4E55 — about 2.1:1, deliberately below a text threshold. It is a hint, not content.
  • Only 2 of 9 cards carry a serial and 4 of 9 a parallel. The five-line reveal is the rare case, not the normal one.
