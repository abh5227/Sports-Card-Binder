Round 4 — the spread

Four frames, same eighteen cards, same page contents. Everything settled in Rounds 1–3 is constant: 2px gutters, dim empty pockets, one sheet of plastic per sheet, 90% scrim, specular-only occupied sleeves, the directional lip, 150px cards.

This bar is 150 px — one card. If it does not match, your zoom is not 100%.

The sheen model has changed since the first render, and it is not a choice on this page. Round 4 shipped one identical band per sheet, which measured 62% across on both pages at L 0.01932 each — the Round 3 identical-speculars bug one level up. Two replacements were built and measured in round4-sheen.html. Model 2, the shallow V, is used here: left peak L 0.02291 at 40% of the spread, right peak L 0.01662 at 65%, ratio 1.38 with the left brighter, and the largest step at the spine. A flat continuous sweep has only one band, so it either sits on the gutter or leaves a whole sheet unlit.

Constant across all four, so do not read them as choices: the pages are flat, no curve — a real binder curves toward the spine and it is a strong book signifier, but it would distort the cards nearest the gutter and the cards are the point. Margins are mirrored: 20px inner, 12px outer, so inner + spine + inner is the visual gutter. The sheen restarts at each sheet in the same direction — the discontinuity at the spine is deliberate.

The cards are now interleaved. The first render put six modern cards on the left page and nine vintage on the right, which left both pages reading as cream and may have flattered a dark frame. Ten modern scans and eight 1887 Allen & Ginter are now mixed across both pages, with a dark modern card against the frame edge on each — Jones at the left page's outer corner, Judge at the right page's. The vintage cards are natively 0.53–0.60 aspect against the pocket's 0.714 and are fitted by height with their own cream border clamped outward; nothing is cropped.

A — Pages only

Two sheets, a spine gap, nothing else. The planner's position: binder-ness was already earned by the pockets and the sheen, so drawing a body is redundant signalling that costs permanent screen area. The sheets keep a contact shadow, without which they are not sheets.

B — Minimal frame

Pages plus a thin surrounding board and a recessed spine. No rings.

C — Full binder

Body, spine, rings, punched pages. The earliest mock's direction — but now with real pockets and a real sheen underneath it, which it never had. Built sympathetically, because it is the direct test of whether the minimal position removes what was working.

D — The other pages

No covers, no board, no rings. The current spread, punched, with the edges of the other pages visible beyond it and fanning into the spine. The stack is driven by a real index against a real total — here spread 4 of 12 — not by a fixed thickness.

D's stack is information, not decoration

The same frame at three states. Thickness left and right reports where you are; total thickness reports how much is under the current sort. No other variant carries this.

The threshold — shown once, on D

A 9-pocket spread is six cards wide. Below the width it needs, the layout drops to a single page rather than shrinking cards: 150px is true binder scale and every judgement in three rounds assumed it — the scrim's 32.7% coverage, the 7.5px type, the name-legibility argument, the empty-pocket brightness. Shrink the cards and all of it is silently re-decided.
