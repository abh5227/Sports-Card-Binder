Round 1 — the card

Nine real card scans, with their real flaws. Four proposals for what the interface adds around one. Every card is 150 × 210 CSS px — the production size. The single and the group answer different questions: the single asks whether the treatment competes with the card, the group asks whether nine unlike cards read as a collection or as noise.

This bar is 150 px — exactly one card wide. If it does not match the cards below, your browser zoom is not at 100% and you are not judging true size.

ABare

Soft shadow to sit it in space, 2px corner radius matching a real card corner. Nothing else. The argument: Topps and Panini already solved the border, nameplate and typography, and at 150px there is no room to add anything that would not compete.

DFramed and labelled

The maximalist, built sympathetically rather than as a straw man: a real mat with a hairline edge, a caption rail divided by a rule, and badges as quiet brass outlines rather than coloured chips. Every card gets the same frame, so the page is consistent. This is the direct test of whether harmonising the cards flattens what makes a collection interesting.

Colour, type and what I changed

  • --ink #E8E4DC on desk 13.34:1, on page 13.76:1 — AAA.
  • --ledger #A8845C on desk 4.93:1 — passes AA for normal text, fails AAA, and fails AA outright if used at small sizes as the only carrier of meaning. Kept for badges and the metadata line in B, where it is redundant with the card itself.
  • Added --ink-dim #9A968E (desk 5.74:1, page 5.92:1). The brief's secondary colour was --ledger, but brass on graphite at 6.5px was doing two jobs — hierarchy and accent — and losing both. A neutral dim grey carries hierarchy; brass is now reserved for things that are actually about the collection (badges, serials).
  • Added --mat #20242A (1.09:1 vs desk) and --hair #2E333A (1.33:1). Surfaces and edges only, never text — those ratios are far below any text threshold and are recorded so nobody later mistakes them for text colours.
  • Type as specified — condensed uppercase, wide tracking, tabular figures, player at 9px and secondary at 6.5px. See the written report for what 6.5px actually does at this size; it is the one part of the brief I would change.
