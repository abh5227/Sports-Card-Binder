The locked design — all five rounds assembled

Every settled decision from Rounds 1–5 on one page, together, for the first time. Nothing here is new and nothing here is proposed. Each round was judged inside its own round; this is the first time they have had to hold as one object.

This bar is 150 px — one card at 9-pocket. If it does not match, your zoom is not 100%.

What to check. Round 4's own rule was that any choice assuming a single page gets re-opened once a spread exists. This is that rule one level up.

  1. Open it and flip pages. Both layouts. The turn is T3 at 420 ms — one leaf, double-sided, cards riding in their pockets through the arc.
  2. Put it beside the photograph of the real binder. Which would you rather look at? Round 4 planned this check and never ran it. It is the question the whole design has been aimed at.
  3. Does anything that was right on its own round read wrong beside the other four? The sheen was judged before the frame. The frame was judged carrying a different sheen. The reveal was judged on one card, on one page, two rounds before there was a spread.

9-pocket — browsing

Tight gutter, one sheet of plastic, mirrored margins, minimal frame. Hover any card for the reveal; click to hold it open.

The last spread carries a tail — a page that runs out of cards. Empty pockets are the lit sleeve at the dimmed setting, and the mechanism there is area, not luminance: six contiguous empties are a large uniform region while the cards are busy.

4-pocket — display

The same page at k = 2. Card, sleeve inset, gutter, page margins, scrim padding, scrim type, hairline and lip all scale by the same factor. Nine is unchanged by construction.

At 2× the artwork behind the panel is also 2×, so the 10% that comes through becomes legible. Hover Crosby here — his own nameplate reads through the scrim beside the reveal's text. That was looked at and kept. It is translucency doing its job, not a defect being tolerated.

Where this port diverged from the record

Four things surfaced by assembling it. None is a design change. Three are gaps or errors in the written record; one is an observation for Round 6.

  1. The spine is 18 px, not the 16 px the record states. The Round 4 entry says “settled at 16 px in B”. Both artefacts Andy actually looked at — round4-spread.html and round5-turn.html — use 18 px; 16 px is the :root default that frame A inherited, and B overrides it. Built at 18, because that is what was judged. The record is wrong and needs correcting.
  2. Nothing says whether the frame scales at 4-pocket. Round 3's rule lists card, inset, gutter, page margin, scrim padding, scrim type, hairline and lip — the frame did not exist yet, and Round 4 never revisited 4-pocket. Built with the frame at 1× (board 11 px, spine 18 px) while everything inside the page scales, because B won partly on costing only 40 px and doubling that at 4-pocket would un-decide it. This is a judgement call filling a gap, and it is a looking question.
  3. The card lips stay bright through the turn. The leaf's shading pass sits at z-index: auto, so the lip at 5 punches through it and a card's top-left edge keeps its full brightness while the page swings into shadow. Reproduced exactly as judged rather than quietly fixed — but it is worth a look, because a physical page turning away from a light does not keep bright edges.
  4. The reveal text on the vintage scans is placeholder and will disagree with the card face — the scan may read Timothy Keefe while the panel says Unidentified. The images are real public-domain scans; the metadata is not catalogue data and there is no schema yet. One of them carries the measured worst-case set name so the five-line wrap is present here rather than only in the round that found it.
