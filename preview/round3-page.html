Round 3 — the page

One page. Nine pockets, or four. Card treatment and sleeve treatment are closed and unchanged from Rounds 1 and 2 — specular-only where a card sits, the lit sleeve where none does, a directional lip on the card's edge, scrim on hover.

This bar is 150 px — one card wide at 9-pocket. If it does not match, your zoom is not 100%.

What is deliberately plain here, so you do not react to the wrong thing: the page is a flat rectangle of #171A1F with a constant 18px margin and square corners. There is no binder body, no board, no rings, no shadow under the page, no second page and no gutter between pages. All of that is Round 4. Do not judge the frame — judge what is inside it.

1 — The open question: how tight are the gutters?

Three widths, full 9-pocket page, nine real cards, everything else identical. The page margin is held constant at 18px so that only the gutter varies. Card-to-card distance is the gutter plus twice the 7px sleeve inset, so it is always 14px more than the number quoted.

The same three gaps, adjacent

One row from each page, side by side, for the fine judgement the stacked pages cannot give you.

2 — One plastic sheet, or nine

Visible only with nine pockets on screen at once: Round 2 gave every pocket its own identical specular, because it only ever tested one pocket. Nine identical highlights are not what a sheet of plastic under one light does. Left, the page as Round 2 would have built it; right, one sheen across the whole sheet, crossing the gutters as a real sheet does.

3 — The tail: three cards, six empty

The real tail state, and where the empty-pocket salience limit gets settled. The planner's recorded expectation — reasoning, therefore the weakest kind of entry — is that the Round 2 setting was tuned against a lone pocket in isolation and needs far less brightness surrounded by cards. Four settings, dimmest to Round 2's.

4 — 4-pocket, cards at 2×

Nine is browsing; four is display. The cards get bigger rather than the page getting smaller. UI type does not scale — the scrim's body text is 7.5px in both, so at 4-pocket it occupies roughly half the relative height, which is what makes worst-case scrim coverage a nine-pocket-only problem.

5 — The scrim up, dark card, full page

One card hovered among eight that are not.

6 — The worst-case set name at 9-pocket

The longest of 1,210 real set names, 48 characters, on a card in a full page. This is where the 40–48% coverage recorded at Round 1 actually lives.

7 — Does “shrink to content” survive at 9-pocket?

The 4-pocket band is mostly empty, and shrinking it to its content fixes that. But Round 1 settled a full-width scrim at 9-pocket, so before that becomes a rule it has to be checked at the size where it was settled — including on a card with very little to say.
